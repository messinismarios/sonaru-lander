## The problem it fixes

If you follow a handful of artists, your streaming app keeps up fine. Follow a few hundred
and it quietly stops. The home screen is not a list of what came out, it is a ranking of
what an algorithm expects you to play, and a ranking never tells you what it left out. A
single released the same Friday as four bigger records, an EP from someone you have not
played in a while: easy for a ranker to skip, and exactly the thing you wanted to hear.

## What Sonaru does

You tell Sonaru which artists you care about. From then on:

- **Every release lands in one feed**, newest first. Albums, EPs, singles. No algorithm
  decides what you see, so when you reach the end, you really are caught up.
- **You get a notification the moment it drops**, for the artists you choose. The ones you
  would drop everything for can tell you straight away, and the rest can stay quiet.
- **New music can go straight into a playlist** on your streaming service, so it is
  waiting for you before you open anything.

## What it is not

Sonaru is not a streaming service, and you do not have to switch. It works alongside
Spotify and Apple Music: Sonaru tells you what came out, and the subscription you already
have stays as is.

## What it costs

The feed, the notifications and auto-add to a playlist are free, and they stay free.
