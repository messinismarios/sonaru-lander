## The short answer

Follow the artist on Spotify, then check the **What's New** bell at the top of Home. That is
the closest Spotify comes to a list of every new release from the artists you follow. What
Spotify does not offer is a push notification when one of them puts out new music: its
[notification settings](https://support.spotify.com/us/article/notification-settings/) let
you get notified of new podcast episodes, and list nothing equivalent for an artist's new
songs.

## What Spotify gives you today

### Following an artist

Following is the switch everything else hangs off. Spotify's own help page puts it plainly:
follow artists
"[to never miss a new release](https://support.spotify.com/us/article/follow-artists-podcasts-and-shows/)
and to improve Spotify recommendations." Open the artist's profile and tap **Follow**. Liking
their songs is not the same thing, and it does not put them in the places below.

### What's New, behind the bell

What's New is
"[a feed that gathers all the new releases from the artists and shows you follow](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2021-07-26/spotifys-whats-new-feed-means-youll-never-miss-the-latest-releases-from-the-shows-and-artists-you-follow/)",
reached from the bell icon at the top of the Home tab. Spotify says it updates in real time,
and it has a filter to show music only, so podcast episodes stay out of the way.

It is the best of the three. The catch is in how you use it: it waits for you. Nothing
arrives on your lock screen; a release sits in the list until you think to open it.

### The Following feed

In July 2025 Spotify added a
[Following feed for music](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-07-30/4-must-try-features-that-make-spotify-the-best-place-to-be-a-fan/),
which "brings music from the artists you follow into one convenient destination", where you
can preview a new track, play it, or save it for later. Like What's New, it is somewhere you
go, not something that comes to you.

### Release Radar

Release Radar is the one most people know, and the one most people mean when they say
Spotify told them about a new song. It is a playlist, and three details from
[Spotify's own description](https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/getting-music-on-release-radar/)
explain why it misses things:

- **It updates once a week, on Friday.** A single that came out on a Tuesday waits until
  Friday to reach you.
- **You get one song per artist per week.** If an artist you follow puts out a ten-track
  album, Release Radar picks one of them.
- **It is not only the artists you follow.** It mixes them with artists you listen to and
  "other artists we think they'll like". In July 2026 Spotify
  [gave it more personalised recommendations and new controls](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-07-10/discovery-playlists-release-radar-control-updates/),
  which makes it a better discovery playlist and no closer to being a complete list.

None of that is a flaw in Release Radar. It is built to help you discover music, and it is
good at that. It is just not built to tell you about everything the artists you follow put
out.

## Where the gaps are

Put the three together and a pattern shows. Spotify knows the moment an artist you follow
releases something, and it records it in two feeds and a playlist. But:

- **Nothing notifies you.** Every one of these is a place you have to remember to check.
- **The one that comes to you is weekly and partial.** Release Radar arrives on Friday with
  one song per artist, alongside music you did not ask for.
- **The more artists you follow, the worse it gets.** With a dozen artists, glancing at the
  bell now and then is enough. With a few hundred, the feed scrolls past faster than you
  check it, and the release you actually cared about goes by between two you did not.

That last one is the whole problem. I got tired of missing songs from my favorite artists,
and none of Spotify's places was ever going to fix it, because none of them is designed to.

## Making the most of what Spotify has

Before reaching for anything else, these help:

1. **Follow, do not just like.** Go through the artists you care about and tap Follow on each
   one. Everything above is driven by follows.
2. **Open the bell, not Release Radar,** when you want to know what is new. Filter it to
   music. It is the complete list; Release Radar is a selection.
3. **Check on release day.** Most new music comes out on Friday, so a Friday look at What's
   New catches the bulk of it. It will still miss the Tuesday single.
